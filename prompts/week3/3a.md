# Goal

Determine which geometry approach can provide **range whose spatial shape is stable and accurate enough for later underwater restoration**.

The downstream use is range-dependent:

- backscatter estimation/removal,
- wavelength-dependent attenuation inversion.

This is **not** a reconstruction-quality competition.

The desired output of Week 3 is ultimately a usable per-frame range product plus a measured error budget.

Phase 3A is the bounded geometry bakeoff that determines which geometry family should supply or constrain that range.

The central question is:

> Given normal diver-held underwater video, which geometry approach produces the most trustworthy spatial range structure for restoration?

---

# Critical conceptual priority

Global scale error is low priority.

For:

```text
I = J * exp(-β_att * d)
    + B_inf * (1 - exp(-β_bs * d))
```

a global transformation:

```text
d -> s*d
β -> β/s
```

is absorbable when β is freely fitted within a clip.

Therefore:

> prioritize spatially varying range/shape error far above global metric-scale error.

Do not treat lack of absolute metric scale as failure.

Do not claim metric accuracy without independent evidence.

A geometry field that is correct up to one global scale may be fully adequate for later restoration.

## Range

Define the comparison quantities **before implementing cross-system comparisons**.

### Central / pinhole methods

Distinguish explicitly:

```text
z-depth
    = coordinate along the camera optical axis

ray range
    = Euclidean distance from the camera center to the 3D scene point
```

Given a calibrated central camera and ray direction, derive the conversion between these quantities explicitly and test it synthetically.

Use **ray range / Euclidean camera-to-point distance** as the primary central-camera comparison quantity unless a method's native semantics require otherwise.

Do not label z-depth as range.

### Refractive / non-central geometry

Do **not** mechanically reuse the central-camera definition.

A flat-port refractive observation consists of piecewise ray segments through different media and generally does not share one common camera center.

Distinguish, where the refractive implementation exposes enough information:

```text
total geometric path length
water-segment path length
central-camera-equivalent range, if used only as an approximation
```

For later underwater attenuation/backscatter physics, the physically relevant quantity is the **path length through water**.

For Phase 3A cross-family comparison, if exact water-path range cannot be extracted consistently from the refractive implementation:

- preserve the refractive method's native 3D geometry,
- compare spatial shape at co-visible observations without pretending a central-camera conversion is exact,
- document any approximation explicitly,
- do not fabricate a common range quantity merely to simplify evaluation.

Before proceeding to Stage 6, document for every method:

- native depth/range definition,
- coordinate frame,
- pose convention,
- conversion applied,
- assumptions required,
- whether the resulting comparison quantity is exact or approximate.

Add synthetic tests for every project-owned conversion.

# Stage 6 — Cross-family comparison

This is the most important part of Phase 3A.

Do not ask:

> which reconstruction looks coolest?

Ask:

> do the systems agree on the spatial range structure that later restoration will consume?

---

## Global-scale alignment

Because global scale is not the primary concern, allow **one global scale factor** when comparing two geometry/range estimates where appropriate.

### Scale-fitting scope

For a given:

```text
method pair
+
clip
```

fit **one scale jointly over eligible comparison observations across the evaluated clip/range**.

Do not fit a separate scale independently per frame for the primary comparison.

Record:

- fitted scale,
- robust fitting procedure,
- observations used.

Per-frame scale fits may be inspected **only as a diagnostic for scale drift**.

Do not use per-frame scale normalization to hide temporal scale instability.

Do not allow in the primary comparison:

- local spatial warping,
- per-region scale,
- nonlinear depth remapping,
- arbitrary per-frame rescaling.

These may be used only as explicitly labeled diagnostics where scientifically justified.

The main comparison must preserve spatial-shape differences.

---

## Dense vs sparse

Evaluate dense learned geometry against sparse classical/refractive observations only where a valid observation relationship exists.

For each eligible sparse observation:

1. identify the corresponding image/frame observation,
2. use the reconstruction's documented camera model / observation coordinates,
3. sample the dense method's range at that corresponding image location,
4. apply explicit bounds, validity, confidence and non-finite handling.

Do **not** nearest-neighbor-match unrelated reconstructed 3D point clouds in world coordinates and call that correspondence.

For refractive observations, use the fork's actual observation/projection/ray semantics where available rather than silently replacing them with a pinhole projection.

Compare dense methods separately against:

```text
ordinary SfM
refractive SfM
```

Do not treat either as ground truth.

Report:

- robust relative error after one clip-level global scale,
- median error,
- useful percentiles,
- error versus image radius,
- error versus estimated range,
- spatial residual visualizations,
- per-frame and cross-frame variation.

If ordinary and refractive sparse geometry disagree, preserve that disagreement.

Do not average them into pseudo-ground-truth.

---

## Dense vs dense

Compare learned dense systems after one clip-level global alignment.

Inspect:

- spatial residuals,
- near/mid/far behavior,
- frame-to-frame consistency,
- scale drift,
- confidence-vs-disagreement/error relationships where possible.

High cross-model agreement is evidence of **consistency**, not proof of correctness.

# Stage 7 — Restoration-oriented range sensitivity proxy

Do not implement full Week 5/6 restoration.

Phase 3A should quantify whether observed geometry disagreement is likely to matter downstream.

Use a bounded analytical/synthetic sensitivity study based on the project's physical model.

For representative β and range regimes, examine how perturbations such as:

```text
global scale error
local ±5%
local ±10%
local ±20%
range-dependent bias
radial spatial bias
```

affect:

```text
t = exp(-β*d)
```

and the magnitude/sensitivity of later inversion.

The goal is **not** to choose final β.

The goal is to establish an approximate error budget:

> what kinds and magnitudes of range error appear negligible, tolerable, or dangerous for later restoration?

Use this to judge whether observed geometry differences matter.

Do not begin actual backscatter estimation/removal or attenuation inversion.

# Stage 8 — Decide what evidence is missing

At the end of the bakeoff, classify the outcome explicitly.

## Outcome A — strong convergence

If several fundamentally different approaches agree closely in spatial shape and disagreement falls below restoration-relevant sensitivity:

> geometry is likely good enough for the next phase.

No controlled C2 acquisition is required immediately.

## Outcome B — acquisition-limited

If methods fail because existing footage lacks parallax/overlap or is too dynamic:

> recommend a future C1 geometry clip.

Do not redesign algorithms first.

## Outcome C — camera-model ambiguity

If ordinary and refractive geometry differ materially in spatial shape and learned methods do not resolve the disagreement:

> C2 independent geometry/range measurements become high-value.

## Outcome D — domain-shift failure

If general learned geometry degrades underwater and Wat3R materially repairs it:

> record underwater adaptation as an important geometry axis.

## Outcome E — matching failure

If B materially repairs A while the resulting geometry otherwise agrees:

> correspondence was the classical bottleneck.

## Outcome F — no single winner

If one method has superior dense local shape and another superior poses/global consistency:

> recommend a compositional geometry path.

Do not manufacture a single-system winner.

# Stop rule

Phase 3A is a bounded bakeoff.

Do not add additional geometry systems merely because:

- a newer paper exists,
- another model ranks higher on a benchmark,
- a repository looks interesting.

The predeclared systems are enough.

A new candidate may be added only if:

1. a **specific observed failure** is identified,
2. none of A–F tests that failure hypothesis,
3. the candidate directly addresses that named failure,
4. adding it is bounded.

Otherwise stop.

Do not turn Week 3 into a geometry-model survey.
